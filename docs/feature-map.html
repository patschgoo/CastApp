CastApp · how everything fits together

CastApp Feature Map

CastApp is the Android app that sends your phone's screen, its videos, music and photos, network folders, Plex and Jellyfin, TV channels and web videos to the TVs and speakers in your home, and works as their remote. The same app on a Fire TV, Shield or Android TV box becomes CastApp TV, a receiver with lower delay and extras. No ads, no tracking, no account.

Walk-throughs

How the parts work together

Mirror the phone screen to the Shield

H1 → B1 → A1 → H3 → A5 → A4
  1. Open CastApp on the Shield once. CastApp TV shows "Ready to cast" and its address.
  2. On the phone, Cast to lists the Shield with a CastApp TV label.
  3. Tap Mirror screen and confirm Android's "Start casting?".
  4. The phone encodes the screen; the Shield plays it with almost no buffer, aiming for under half a second.
  5. On weak Wi-Fi the quality drops by itself; Quality on the casting card sets it by hand.
  6. Stop from the cast bar, the notification or the tile.

An anime episode from Plex on the Shield's CastApp TV

D1 → D4 → D6 → D7 → H9 → D5
  1. Sign in to Plex once with the code at plex.tv/link.
  2. Pick the episode and the Shield. CastApp TV gets the MKV file itself; nothing is converted.
  3. CastApp picks the subtitle track in your Subtitle languages (the full one, not "Signs & Songs") and tells the TV; the sound track picked in Plex goes along too.
  4. The TV shows the track in your subtitle look. The picker switches tracks or turns them off.
  5. If the Shield can't decode the picture, it says why and CastApp falls back to Plex's converted stream.
  6. Resume point and watched mark go back to Plex; the next episodes are queued.

A file from a network share on a Chromecast

C4 → G2 → G1 → F1 → F3
  1. Network & cloud → +: paste the share address from File Explorer, user and password (stored encrypted).
  2. Tap an episode: the folder becomes the play list, in counted order.
  3. The phone reads the file from the share and serves it to the Chromecast, with seeking.
  4. An H.264 MKV or recording is repackaged; HEVC, MPEG-2 or AVI is converted to H.264 and AAC.
  5. A subtitle file in your language goes along; look and timing can be changed.

Another app sends a play list

L2 → L3 → L4
  1. The app builds the list (addresses, titles, subtitles, poster, sound and subtitle language) with a start position and a request id.
  2. CastApp shows its Cast to TV sheet; you tap a TV. No automation switch is needed for this.
  3. CastApp casts the list and answers ok or cancelled.
  4. PLAYBACK reports go to that app only: started, progress about every 30 s, paused, stopped, finished or failed with a reason.
  5. The app saves resume points and marks an episode watched on finished.

Record a TV channel

J1 → J2 → J4 → J6 → J7
  1. Add a channel list (link, file or Xtream log-in) and a guide.
  2. In the Grid, tap a programme → Record, or long-press a channel for Record now.
  3. Choose where: the app's folder, the Movies gallery or a saved network folder.
  4. The recording runs in the background with a notification and a few minutes of padding. CastApp warns if the same log-in is already in use.
  5. On Android 12+, a planned recording may need a tap on its notification unless CastApp is excluded from battery optimization.
  6. Recordings and reminders lists it to play on the TV, stop or delete.

Control a TV with the remote

K4 → K1 → K2 → K3
  1. Tap the remote icon at the top, a TV's ⋮ menu, or Use this phone as the remote in the casting card.
  2. Google TV or Shield: type the code the TV shows. Samsung: tap Allow on the TV. LG: accept on the TV. Roku: nothing.
  3. D-pad, volume, channels, Back, Home, Menu and Mute work; the mic button searches by voice on Google TV and Shield.
  4. Fire TV: pair the phone as a Bluetooth remote. Phones with an IR blaster: point it at the TV and pick the brand.
Ground rules

What CastApp always keeps to

TVs and other devices

  • Casting to TVs without CastApp keeps working unchanged. CastApp-to-CastApp extras (low-latency player, swap, join, sync, hand-over) use short timeouts and fall back silently.
  • The small servers on the phone only run while you cast, and every address carries a random token.
  • CastApp TV and the peer server between CastApp devices only answer devices on the local network.
  • Screen mirroring always needs your OK in Android's prompt; no app can answer it.
  • No Google Play services needed, except for the optional watch app.

Your accounts and data

  • No ads, no tracking, no account.
  • Passwords and sign-ins (network folders, Plex and Jellyfin, AirPlay pairings, OpenSubtitles, network remotes, internet TV list and guide addresses with their Xtream log-in) and the Continue watching history are encrypted with the Android Keystore. The parental PIN is stored only as a salted hash.
  • Android's automatic backup and phone-to-phone transfer leave out everything internet TV keeps (channel lists, favourites, reminders, planned recordings: their addresses can hold a log-in) and the recordings themselves (too big for the backup).
  • Plex and Jellyfin: CastApp reads; it only reports playback and watched marks, and never changes Plex's saved subtitle choice.
  • Network shares: CastApp reads; the only thing it writes there is a TV recording you send to that folder.
  • Automation is off by default; playback reports only go to the app that asked.

How it's built

  • Every text in English and German (informal "du"); texts sent to TVs, servers or other phones stay English.
  • Runs from Android 7.1 (Fire OS 6); newer Android features are guarded.
  • Protocols are tested against fakes over real network sockets, including pyatv's AirPlay pairing.
  • No real names, addresses, share names or drive IDs in the repository; examples use 192.168.1.x and "Living room TV".

Known risks and gaps

  • The Shield has no decoder for 10-bit H.264 (Hi10P, common for anime). CastApp TV says so and the phone tries to convert, but that needs a 10-bit H.264 decoder on the phone, which many phones lack. Then Plex's converted stream is the way, or from the PC AniBox's TV copies: it converts such files to 8-bit H.264 on the PC and sends those to CastApp TV (Blu-ray .m2ts files it only repackages into MKV).
  • On Android 10+ TVs (Fire OS 8, recent Google TV) CastApp TV opens its player by itself only if it may display over other apps; Fire TVs need a one-time adb command or the app kept open.
  • AirPlay was tested only against pyatv's code; Roku is experimental. YouTube's lounge protocol is unofficial and can change.
  • Plex and Jellyfin were written against the documented APIs and simulated servers; the converting parameters may need changes on real servers.
